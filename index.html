Forever Young

About the Song

“Forever Young” is a synth-pop classic by the German band Alphaville, released in 1984. The song quickly became one of their most beloved tracks, known for its catchy melody and nostalgic lyrics reflecting youth and the passage of time.

It was a major hit in Europe and German-speaking countries, and later gained attention in the United States, reaching the charts in 1988. The music video, filmed in the United Kingdom, was directed by Brian Ward. As of November 2025, “Forever Young” has over 1.1 billion streams on Spotify.

Many artists have covered this song, and it even inspired Jay-Z's track “Young Forever.” Written in the key of C major, the song continues to resonate with fans around the world.

About the Artist

Alphaville was formed in 1981 when lead singer Marian Gold met Bernhard Lloyd in West Berlin. The band's early sound was influenced by British synth and indie acts, including Tubeway Army, Gary Numan, and Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark.

Their first single, “Big in Japan,” was written by Gold in 1979, before the band officially formed. Initially, they called themselves “Forever Young” but later adopted the name “Alphaville,” inspired by the 1965 science fiction film. Together with Frank Mertens, Gold and Lloyd created the demo for “Forever Young,” which later became a hit. Alphaville played their first concert on December 31, 1982, in Enger, Westphalia.
